Verified historical records

December 1943

A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity

Warren S. McCulloch and Walter Pitts published A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity in The Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics in December 1943, describing neural activity in terms of propositional logic and formal networks.

March 1950

Programming a Computer for Playing Chess

Claude E. Shannon published Programming a Computer for Playing Chess in Philosophical Magazine in March 1950, describing how a general-purpose computer could be programmed to play chess through move selection and position evaluation rather than exhaustive enumeration of every possible continuation.

1950

Computing Machinery and Intelligence

Alan Turing published Computing Machinery and Intelligence in Mind, framing machine intelligence through the imitation game and asking whether machines can think.

1955

Dartmouth Artificial Intelligence Proposal

John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon authored the proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, dated August 31, 1955.

Summer 1956

Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence

In the summer of 1956, a small group of scientists gathered at Dartmouth College for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, a research workshop organized under John McCarthy's leadership.

1956

The Logic Theory Machine: A Complex Information Processing System

Carnegie Mellon University Archives preserves the September 1956 article by Allen Newell and Herbert A. Simon titled 'The Logic Theory Machine: A Complex Information Processing System,' published in IRE Transactions on Information Theory, volume IT-2, issue 3.

1958

The Perceptron

Frank Rosenblatt published The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and Organization in the Brain in Psychological Review.

July 31, 1959

Some Studies in Machine Learning Using the Game of Checkers

Arthur L. Samuel published 'Some Studies in Machine Learning Using the Game of Checkers' in the IBM Journal of Research and Development on July 31, 1959. The paper reports two machine-learning procedures investigated through a checkers program and describes experiments in which the program improved its play through learning procedures.

April 1960

Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation by Machine, Part I

John McCarthy published 'Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation by Machine, Part I' in April 1960. The paper describes LISP, developed for the IBM 704 by the Artificial Intelligence group at MIT, and presents a system for representing partial recursive functions over symbolic expressions, including S-expressions and S-functions.

3 May 1960

Empirical Explorations of the Geometry Theorem Machine

Herbert Gelernter, J. R. Hansen and D. W. Loveland published a conference paper documenting empirical work with an IBM 704 geometry theorem-proving machine. The paper reports that the system had proved its first elementary Euclidean plane-geometry theorem in early spring 1959 and subsequently solved numerous textbook and examination problems.

15 December 1964

DENDRAL-64 — Part I: Notational Algorithm for Tree Structures

Joshua Lederberg's DENDRAL-64 technical report, Part I, was published on December 15, 1964. The report documents a system for computer construction, enumeration and notation of organic molecules as tree structures and cyclic graphs, with Part I devoted to a notational algorithm for tree structures.

1965

ELIZA source code and DOCTOR script documented

MIT Libraries preserves a 1965 archival item described as a complete printout of the source code listing of ELIZA, written in the MAD-SLIP programming language, with the DOCTOR script attached.

April 5, 1965

DENDRAL Documented in Stanford Machine-Induction Research Memo

On April 5, 1965, Edward A. Feigenbaum and R. W. Watson issued An Initial Problem Statement for a Machine Induction Research Project at the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Project. The memo described research then getting under way on machine induction using organic chemistry and explicitly documented a system called Dendral as an existing ALGOL program for the B-5000.

1966

Shakey the Robot

SRI began the Shakey research program in 1966. Shakey combined mobile robotics with perception, reasoning, planning and route-finding in a single research system developed at SRI's Artificial Intelligence Center.

January 1966

ELIZA—A Computer Program for the Study of Natural Language Communication Between Man and Machine

Joseph Weizenbaum published ELIZA—A Computer Program for the Study of Natural Language Communication Between Man and Machine in Communications of the ACM in January 1966, documenting ELIZA operating within MIT's MAC time-sharing system and conducting natural-language dialogue through keyword-triggered decomposition rules.

1967

Some methods for classification and analysis of multivariate observations

J. MacQueen published 'Some methods for classification and analysis of multivariate observations' in the Proceedings of the Fifth Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability in 1967. The paper describes a process for partitioning an N-dimensional population into k sets and explicitly calls the process 'k-means.'

1970

SHRDLU development period completed

Terry Winograd later documented that he developed SHRDLU at MIT between 1968 and 1970. SHRDLU was a natural-language conversation program operating with a simulated robot arm in a tabletop blocks world, where it could answer questions, carry out commands, and incorporate new facts about that world.

1972

SHRDLU documented in Winograd's 1972 rewritten account

In a later first-person account, Terry Winograd states that his original SHRDLU dissertation was Winograd (1971) and that Winograd (1972) was a rewritten version. The same paper describes SHRDLU as answering questions, carrying out commands, incorporating new facts about its world, and operating in a simulated blocks world.

1973

WABOT-1 — 1973 Waseda Completion Milestone

Waseda University records that Professor Ichiro Kato's team completed WABOT-1 in 1973. University histories describe the humanoid robot as capable of bipedal walking and simple conversation; later Waseda material also describes object grasping and simple Japanese communication.

1974

MYCIN thesis milestone

Edward H. Shortliffe's Stanford thesis documented MYCIN as a rule-based computer program designed to advise physicians regarding antimicrobial therapy selection. Stanford's computer-science report bibliography lists the thesis in October 1974 and describes MYCIN's consultation, explanation, and rule-acquisition components.

August 1975

MYCIN — 1975 Clinical Therapeutics Publication

In August 1975, Edward H. Shortliffe, Randall Davis, Stanton G. Axline, Bruce G. Buchanan, C. Cordell Green, and Stanley N. Cohen published a paper describing progress on MYCIN, an interactive computer program designed to advise physicians about selecting antimicrobial therapy for hospital patients with bacterial infections.

1977

Maximum Likelihood from Incomplete Data Via the EM Algorithm

A. P. Dempster, N. M. Laird, and D. B. Rubin published "Maximum Likelihood from Incomplete Data Via the EM Algorithm" in the Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B in 1977. The paper presents a broadly applicable algorithm for computing maximum-likelihood estimates from incomplete data and derives monotonic-likelihood and convergence results.

April 1980

Neocognitron

Kunihiko Fukushima published Neocognitron: A self-organizing neural network model for a mechanism of pattern recognition unaffected by shift in position in Biological Cybernetics in April 1980. The paper proposes a self-organizing neural-network model for visual pattern recognition and explicitly gives the network the nickname neocognitron.

January 1982

Self-organized formation of topologically correct feature maps

Teuvo Kohonen published 'Self-organized formation of topologically correct feature maps' in Biological Cybernetics in January 1982. The paper studied and simulated a self-organizing process in which signal representations become arranged into topologically ordered output responses.

April 1982

Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities

John J. Hopfield published Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences in April 1982. The paper described a model in which collective properties of many simple neuron-like components produce content-addressable memory through asynchronous parallel processing.

January 1985

A Learning Algorithm for Boltzmann Machines

David H. Ackley, Geoffrey E. Hinton, and Terrence J. Sejnowski published "A Learning Algorithm for Boltzmann Machines" in Cognitive Science in January 1985. The paper describes a general parallel search method based on statistical mechanics and a learning rule for modifying connection strengths to incorporate task-domain knowledge.

1986

Induction of decision trees

J. R. Quinlan published 'Induction of decision trees' in Machine Learning in 1986. The paper summarizes an approach to synthesizing decision trees and describes the ID3 system in detail, including studies addressing noisy or incomplete information.

9 October 1986

Learning representations by back-propagating errors

David E. Rumelhart, Geoffrey E. Hinton and Ronald J. Williams published Learning representations by back-propagating errors in Nature on 9 October 1986, describing a learning procedure that repeatedly adjusts connection weights in networks of neuron-like units to reduce the difference between actual and desired outputs.

1988

Learning to Predict by the Methods of Temporal Differences

Richard S. Sutton published 'Learning to Predict by the Methods of Temporal Differences' in Machine Learning in 1988. The paper introduces a class of incremental prediction-learning procedures that assign credit using differences between temporally successive predictions and studies convergence and optimality in special cases.

May 1989

Learning from Delayed Rewards

Christopher John Cornish Hellaby Watkins submitted his University of Cambridge PhD thesis 'Learning from Delayed Rewards' in May 1989. Watkins's own account states that the thesis described a range of reinforcement-learning algorithms, including one-step Q-learning, and sketched a proof of its convergence.

May 1992

Q-learning

Christopher J. C. H. Watkins and Peter Dayan published Q-learning in Machine Learning in May 1992. The paper describes Q-learning as an incremental dynamic-programming method for agents to improve action-value estimates and presents a detailed convergence theorem for the method under stated conditions.

22 October 1993

TD-Gammon — AAAI Fall Symposium Publication Milestone

Gerald Tesauro published a TD-Gammon paper for AAAI-FS 1993 on 22 October 1993. IBM Research describes TD-Gammon as a neural network that learned to play backgammon through self-play using the TD(lambda) reinforcement-learning algorithm and reports that a version with hand-crafted input features was estimated to play at a strong master level.

1995

A Decision-Theoretic Generalization of On-Line Learning and an Application to Boosting

Yoav Freund and Robert E. Schapire published "A Decision-Theoretic Generalization of On-Line Learning and an Application to Boosting" in the 1995 EuroCOLT proceedings. The paper describes a new boosting algorithm derived from a multiplicative weight-update framework that does not require prior knowledge of the weak learner's performance.

September 1995

Support-Vector Networks

Corinna Cortes and Vladimir Vapnik published Support-Vector Networks in the September 1995 issue of Machine Learning. The paper describes the support-vector network as a learning machine for two-group classification that maps input vectors nonlinearly into a high-dimensional feature space and constructs a linear decision surface there, extending the approach to non-separable training data.

11 May 1997

Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov

IBM's Deep Blue defeated reigning world chess champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match completed on 11 May 1997, winning 3.5 to 2.5 under standard tournament time controls.

15 November 1997

Long Short-Term Memory

Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber published Long Short-Term Memory in Neural Computation on 15 November 1997. The paper introduced the gradient-based method called long short-term memory (LSTM), using constant error flow and multiplicative gate units to address long time-lag learning problems in recurrent networks.

30 November 1998

Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition

Yann LeCun, Leon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio, and Patrick Haffner published Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition in Proceedings of the IEEE. The paper reviews gradient-based learning for document recognition, describes convolutional neural networks for two-dimensional shape variability, presents graph transformer networks for globally trained multimodule recognition systems, and documents the LeNet-5 architecture in its experimental treatment.

September 1, 1999

Random Forests--Random Features

Leo Breiman's University of California, Berkeley Statistics technical report 'Random Forests--Random Features' is dated September 1, 1999. The report describes random forests as combinations of tree predictors in which each tree depends on an independently sampled random vector, and discusses random feature selection for tree splitting.

October 2001

Random Forests

Leo Breiman published "Random Forests" in Machine Learning in October 2001. The paper describes random forests as combinations of tree predictors in which each tree depends on a randomly sampled vector, and analyzes forest generalization error in terms of individual-tree strength and correlation.

February 2003

A Neural Probabilistic Language Model

Yoshua Bengio, Réjean Ducharme, Pascal Vincent, and Christian Jauvin published A Neural Probabilistic Language Model in the Journal of Machine Learning Research in February 2003. The paper proposed learning distributed word representations jointly with a neural probability function for word sequences to address generalization limits associated with the curse of dimensionality.

January 2008

Theano Development and Use Began

The authors of the SciPy 2010 paper 'Theano: A CPU and GPU Math Compiler in Python' state that Theano development and use began in January 2008. A later paper by the Theano Development Team independently states that Theano had been actively and continuously developed since 2008.

2009

ImageNet: A Large-Scale Hierarchical Image Database

Jia Deng, Wei Dong, Richard Socher, Li-Jia Li, Kai Li, and Li Fei-Fei published ImageNet: A Large-Scale Hierarchical Image Database in the 2009 IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition proceedings. The paper introduces ImageNet as a large-scale ontology of images built on the WordNet structure and reports a then-current dataset of 12 subtrees, 5,247 synsets, and 3.2 million images.

1 February 2010

scikit-learn First Public Release

The scikit-learn project history records 1 February 2010 as the date of its first public release, after leadership of the project passed in 2010 to Fabian Pedregosa, Gaël Varoquaux, Alexandre Gramfort, and Vincent Michel at INRIA.

2011

Torch7 Described at BigLearn, NIPS Workshop

Ronan Collobert's publication record and Idiap's institutional publication record identify the paper 'Torch7: A Matlab-like Environment for Machine Learning' by Ronan Collobert, Koray Kavukcuoglu, and Clément Farabet as a 2011 BigLearn, NIPS Workshop publication describing Torch7 as a Lua-based numeric computing framework and machine-learning library.

16 February 2011

IBM Watson wins the Jeopardy! challenge

IBM's Watson question-answering system defeated Jeopardy! champions Ken Jennings and Brad Rutter in a televised contest conducted over February 14–16, 2011. IBM's retrospective records Watson's final winning score as USD 77,147, ahead of Jennings and Rutter.

2012

ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks

Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey E. Hinton published ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks in the 2012 Neural Information Processing Systems proceedings, reporting a large deep convolutional neural network trained for ImageNet classification and substantially lower test error than the comparison results described in the paper.

July 3, 2012

Improving neural networks by preventing co-adaptation of feature detectors

Geoffrey E. Hinton, Nitish Srivastava, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Ruslan R. Salakhutdinov submitted the paper 'Improving neural networks by preventing co-adaptation of feature detectors' on July 3, 2012. The paper describes reducing overfitting by randomly omitting feature detectors during training and refers to the method as random dropout.

January 16, 2013

Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space

Tomas Mikolov, Kai Chen, Greg Corrado, and Jeffrey Dean submitted Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space to arXiv on January 16, 2013, describing two model architectures for learning continuous vector representations of words from very large data sets.

19 December 2013

Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning

Volodymyr Mnih, Koray Kavukcuoglu, David Silver, Alex Graves, Ioannis Antonoglou, Daan Wierstra, and Martin Riedmiller submitted Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning on December 19, 2013. The paper presented a convolutional neural network trained with a variant of Q-learning to learn control policies directly from raw pixel input and evaluated the approach on seven Atari 2600 games from the Arcade Learning Environment.

20 December 2013

Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes Research Publication

Diederik P. Kingma and Max Welling submitted 'Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes' on December 20, 2013. The paper presented a stochastic variational inference and learning method for directed probabilistic models with continuous latent variables, including a reparameterized variational-lower-bound estimator that can be optimized with standard stochastic gradient methods.

2014

Generative Adversarial Nets

Ian J. Goodfellow, Jean Pouget-Abadie, Mehdi Mirza, Bing Xu, David Warde-Farley, Sherjil Ozair, Aaron Courville, and Yoshua Bengio published Generative Adversarial Nets in the 2014 Neural Information Processing Systems proceedings, proposing an adversarial framework that trains a generative model and a discriminative model simultaneously.

March 2014

XGBoost v0.1 Repository Milestone

The official XGBoost repository preserves a v0.1 tag whose referenced commit is dated March 2014. The current official repository identifies XGBoost as originating from a research project at the University of Washington.

20 June 2014

Caffe Technical Paper

The Caffe authors recorded the framework in the paper 'Caffe: Convolutional Architecture for Fast Feature Embedding' on June 20, 2014, describing Caffe as a fully open-source deep-learning framework implemented in C++ with CUDA and bindings for Python and MATLAB.

August 2014

GloVe original release

The Stanford NLP Group records August 2014 as the original release of GloVe, prepared by Jeffrey Pennington. The project describes GloVe as an unsupervised learning algorithm for obtaining vector representations for words from aggregated global word-word co-occurrence statistics.

1 September 2014

Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate

Dzmitry Bahdanau, Kyunghyun Cho, and Yoshua Bengio submitted 'Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate' on September 1, 2014. The paper proposed extending encoder-decoder neural machine translation by allowing the model to automatically soft-search for source-sentence parts relevant to predicting each target word rather than relying only on a single fixed-length source representation.

September 4, 2014

Very Deep Convolutional Networks for Large-Scale Image Recognition

Karen Simonyan and Andrew Zisserman submitted Very Deep Convolutional Networks for Large-Scale Image Recognition to arXiv on September 4, 2014. The paper evaluates convolutional-network depth for large-scale image recognition, using very small 3x3 convolution filters and reporting networks with 16 to 19 weight layers.

10 September 2014

Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks

Ilya Sutskever, Oriol Vinyals, and Quoc V. Le submitted 'Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks' on September 10, 2014, presenting an end-to-end sequence-learning approach that uses one multilayer LSTM to encode an input sequence into a fixed-dimensional vector and another deep LSTM to decode the target sequence.

17 September 2014

GoogLeNet / Inception Research Publication

Christian Szegedy and coauthors submitted 'Going Deeper with Convolutions' on September 17, 2014, presenting the Inception deep convolutional architecture and identifying GoogLeNet as a 22-layer incarnation used for ILSVRC 2014.

December 22, 2014

Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization

Diederik P. Kingma and Jimmy Ba submitted 'Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization' on December 22, 2014. The paper introduces Adam, a first-order gradient-based optimization algorithm for stochastic objective functions based on adaptive estimates of lower-order moments.

February 11, 2015

Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift

Sergey Ioffe and Christian Szegedy submitted Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift on February 11, 2015. The paper proposed normalizing layer inputs as part of the model architecture using statistics computed for each training mini-batch, and reported that the method enabled higher learning rates and reduced sensitivity to initialization.

February 25, 2015

Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning (DQN)

Volodymyr Mnih and colleagues published Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning online in Nature on February 25, 2015. The paper describes a deep Q-network (DQN) agent that combines deep neural networks with reinforcement learning and reports evaluation on 49 Atari 2600 games using pixels and game scores as inputs.

March 2015

Keras Initial Public Release

François Chollet's first-party Keras 1.0 announcement states that Keras was initially released in late March 2015. The Keras GitHub repository contains project activity from late March 2015, consistent with that retrospective account.

March 12, 2015

Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics

Jascha Sohl-Dickstein, Eric A. Weiss, Niru Maheswaranathan, and Surya Ganguli submitted 'Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics' on March 12, 2015. The paper describes an iterative forward diffusion process that destroys structure in a data distribution and a learned reverse diffusion process that restores structure.

18 May 2015

U-Net Research Publication

Olaf Ronneberger, Philipp Fischer, and Thomas Brox submitted 'U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation' on May 18, 2015. The paper presented a network and training strategy for biomedical image segmentation built around a contracting path for context and a symmetric expanding path for precise localization.

June 8, 2015

You Only Look Once: Unified, Real-Time Object Detection

Joseph Redmon, Santosh Divvala, Ross Girshick and Ali Farhadi submitted You Only Look Once: Unified, Real-Time Object Detection to arXiv on June 8, 2015. The paper presents YOLO as an object-detection approach that frames detection as a regression problem and uses a single neural network to predict bounding boxes and class probabilities directly from full images in one evaluation.

9 June 2015

Chainer Open-Source Release

Preferred Infrastructure and Preferred Networks announced on June 9, 2015 that they had developed the Chainer deep-learning framework and released it as open-source software.

9 November 2015

TensorFlow Open-Source Release

Google announced TensorFlow on November 9, 2015 and stated that it was open-sourcing the machine-learning system that day.

3 December 2015

MXNet Technical Paper

The MXNet authors submitted the technical paper 'MXNet: A Flexible and Efficient Machine Learning Library for Heterogeneous Distributed Systems' on December 3, 2015, describing MXNet as a multi-language machine-learning library particularly for deep neural networks.

December 10, 2015

Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition (ResNet)

Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren and Jian Sun submitted Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition on December 10, 2015. The paper presents a residual learning framework that reformulates layers as learning residual functions with reference to layer inputs and reports experiments with residual networks up to 152 layers on ImageNet.

2016

PaddlePaddle Open-Source Release

PaddlePaddle's official project documentation and Baidu's corporate reporting state that Baidu open-sourced the PaddlePaddle deep-learning framework in 2016.

25 January 2016

CNTK GitHub Repository Goes Public

Microsoft's CNTK project history records that the CNTK repository on GitHub went public on January 25, 2016, together with a new CNTK website.

January 27, 2016

Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search

David Silver and colleagues published Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search online in Nature on January 27, 2016. The paper described AlphaGo, combining policy and value neural networks with Monte Carlo tree search, and reported that the system defeated European Go champion Fan Hui by five games to zero.

February 4, 2016

Asynchronous Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning

Volodymyr Mnih and collaborators submitted 'Asynchronous Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning' on February 4, 2016. The paper presents asynchronous variants of four reinforcement-learning algorithms and reports that its asynchronous actor-critic variant was the best-performing method in the study.

March 9, 2016

XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System

Tianqi Chen and Carlos Guestrin submitted XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System to arXiv on March 9, 2016. The paper describes XGBoost as a scalable end-to-end tree boosting system and presents a sparsity-aware algorithm, a weighted quantile sketch for approximate tree learning, and systems techniques for scaling beyond billions of examples.

July 21, 2016

Layer Normalization

Jimmy Lei Ba, Jamie Ryan Kiros, and Geoffrey E. Hinton submitted Layer Normalization on July 21, 2016. The paper proposed computing normalization statistics across the summed inputs to the neurons in a layer for a single training case, rather than across a mini-batch, and described the method as straightforward to apply to recurrent neural networks.

8 September 2016

WaveNet: A generative model for raw audio

Google DeepMind publicly presented WaveNet on September 8, 2016 as a deep generative model of raw audio waveforms. The associated research described WaveNet as a probabilistic autoregressive neural network that generates raw audio sample by sample and demonstrated applications including text-to-speech and music generation.

19 January 2017

PyTorch Public Release

The PyTorch team retrospectively marked January 19, 2017 as the date PyTorch was released publicly.

April 17, 2017

MobileNets: Efficient Convolutional Neural Networks for Mobile Vision Applications

Andrew G. Howard, Menglong Zhu, Bo Chen, Dmitry Kalenichenko, Weijun Wang, Tobias Weyand, Marco Andreetto and Hartwig Adam submitted MobileNets: Efficient Convolutional Neural Networks for Mobile Vision Applications on April 17, 2017. The paper presents MobileNets as efficient models for mobile and embedded vision applications, based on depthwise separable convolutions and two global hyperparameters for trading off latency and accuracy.

2017

Attention Is All You Need

Vaswani and colleagues introduced the Transformer architecture in Attention Is All You Need, proposing a sequence-transduction architecture based on attention mechanisms without recurrence or convolutions.

6 September 2017

Apache MXNet 0.11 First Official Incubating Release

Amazon Web Services announced on September 6, 2017 the availability of Apache MXNet 0.11 and described it as MXNet's first official release as an Apache incubating project.

7 September 2017

Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) Announced

Microsoft announced the Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) format on 7 September 2017 in conjunction with Facebook, describing it as a shared model representation intended to support interoperability across AI frameworks.

December 2017

LightGBM: A Highly Efficient Gradient Boosting Decision Tree

Guolin Ke, Qi Meng, Thomas Finley, Taifeng Wang, Wei Chen, Weidong Ma, Qiwei Ye, and Tie-Yan Liu published "LightGBM: A Highly Efficient Gradient Boosting Decision Tree" in December 2017. The paper describes LightGBM as a gradient-boosting decision-tree implementation using Gradient-based One-Side Sampling (GOSS) and Exclusive Feature Bundling (EFB) to improve training efficiency and scalability.

December 5, 2017

Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

David Silver and colleagues submitted Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm to arXiv on December 5, 2017. The paper described AlphaZero as a single algorithm that learned through self-play from random play with no domain knowledge beyond game rules and reported superhuman performance in chess, shogi, and Go.

2018

JAX Open-Source Release

JAX's official repository citation metadata states that 2018 corresponds to the project's open-source release, and Google Research's 2018 retrospective says Google released JAX that year as an accelerator-backed NumPy system supporting automatic differentiation and using XLA infrastructure.

15 February 2018

Deep Contextualized Word Representations

Matthew E. Peters, Mark Neumann, Mohit Iyyer, Matt Gardner, Christopher Clark, Kenton Lee, and Luke Zettlemoyer submitted 'Deep contextualized word representations' on February 15, 2018, introducing deep contextualized word representations learned as functions of the internal states of a pretrained deep bidirectional language model.

June 11, 2018

Improving language understanding with unsupervised learning

OpenAI published Improving language understanding with unsupervised learning on June 11, 2018, describing and releasing a scalable, task-agnostic language system that combined Transformer models with unsupervised pre-training followed by supervised fine-tuning across diverse language tasks.

October 11, 2018

BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding

Jacob Devlin, Ming-Wei Chang, Kenton Lee and Kristina Toutanova submitted BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding on October 11, 2018. The paper introduced BERT, a bidirectional Transformer-based language representation model pretrained from unlabeled text and designed for fine-tuning across downstream natural-language-processing tasks.

2 November 2018

BERT Open-Source Release

Google AI announced the open-source release of BERT on November 2, 2018. The release included TensorFlow source code and pre-trained language representation models. The associated authors' paper describes BERT as Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers and as a method for pre-training deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text.

January 9, 2019

Transformer-XL: Attentive Language Models beyond a Fixed-Length Context

Zihang Dai, Zhilin Yang, Yiming Yang, Jaime Carbonell, Quoc V. Le, and Ruslan Salakhutdinov submitted 'Transformer-XL: Attentive Language Models beyond a Fixed-Length Context' on January 9, 2019. The paper proposes Transformer-XL, a neural architecture using a segment-level recurrence mechanism and a positional encoding scheme to model dependency beyond a fixed-length context.

February 14, 2019

Better Language Models and Their Implications (GPT-2)

OpenAI published Better Language Models and Their Implications on February 14, 2019, introducing GPT-2 as a large transformer-based language model with 1.5 billion parameters and describing an initial staged-release approach in which a much smaller model and technical paper were released while the full trained model was withheld.

April 19, 2019

ERNIE: Enhanced Representation through Knowledge Integration

Yu Sun, Shuohuan Wang, Yukun Li, Shikun Feng, Xuyi Chen, Han Zhang, Xin Tian, Danxiang Zhu, Hao Tian, and Hua Wu submitted the paper 'ERNIE: Enhanced Representation through Knowledge Integration' on April 19, 2019. The paper described ERNIE as a language representation model using knowledge-oriented masking strategies, including entity-level and phrase-level masking.

23 October 2019

T5 Research Publication

Google researchers submitted the T5 paper, 'Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer,' on October 23, 2019, introducing a unified text-to-text framework for transfer learning in NLP.

November 19, 2019

Mastering Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi by Planning with a Learned Model

Julian Schrittwieser and colleagues submitted the paper Mastering Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi by Planning with a Learned Model to arXiv on November 19, 2019. The paper presents the MuZero algorithm, which combines tree-based search with a learned model and reports results across Atari, Go, chess, and shogi without being given the underlying environment dynamics or game rules.

March 13, 2020

Masakhane -- Machine Translation For Africa

Iroro Orife and collaborators submitted 'Masakhane -- Machine Translation For Africa' on March 13, 2020. The paper documents Masakhane as an open-source, continent-wide, distributed, online research effort focused on machine translation for African languages and describes its community-building and research methodology.

28 March 2020

MindSpore Open-Source Release

Huawei announced on March 28, 2020 that its all-scenario AI computing framework MindSpore had been officially open-sourced.

May 22, 2020

Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks

Patrick Lewis and coauthors submitted 'Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks' on May 22, 2020. The paper introduces RAG models that combine a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model as parametric memory with a dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed through a neural retriever as non-parametric memory.

May 28, 2020

Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)

Tom B. Brown and coauthors submitted Language Models are Few-Shot Learners on May 28, 2020. The paper reports GPT-3, an autoregressive language model with 175 billion parameters, and evaluates task-agnostic zero-shot, one-shot and few-shot performance without gradient updates or fine-tuning during those evaluations.

June 19, 2020

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

Jonathan Ho, Ajay Jain, and Pieter Abbeel submitted 'Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models' to arXiv on June 19, 2020. The paper reports high-quality image synthesis using diffusion probabilistic models, with training based on a weighted variational bound connected to denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics.

October 22, 2020

An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale

Alexey Dosovitskiy and coauthors submitted 'An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale' on October 22, 2020. The paper describes applying a pure Transformer directly to sequences of image patches and names the resulting model Vision Transformer (ViT).

January 5, 2021

CLIP: Connecting Text and Images

OpenAI published its CLIP milestone on January 5, 2021, introducing Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training as a neural network that learns visual concepts from natural-language supervision and can be applied to visual classification tasks by specifying category names in natural language.

26 April 2021

PanGu-α Technical Paper Submission
