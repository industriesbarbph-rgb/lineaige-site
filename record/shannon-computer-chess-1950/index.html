Programming a Computer for Playing Chess

Claude E. Shannon published Programming a Computer for Playing Chess in Philosophical Magazine in March 1950, describing how a general-purpose computer could be programmed to play chess through move selection and position evaluation rather than exhaustive enumeration of every possible continuation.

March 1950PREHISTORY / PRECURSORVERIFIED

Historical classification

This verified record predates the 1956 beginning of artificial intelligence as a recognized research field and is preserved as historical prehistory/foundation.

Why it matters

A documented early computer-chess paper that framed chess as a programming and search problem for general-purpose digital computers, providing a historically useful pre-Dartmouth record of machine problem-solving research.
