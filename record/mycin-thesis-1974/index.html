Stanford's bibliography of Artificial Intelligence Laboratory reports identifies Edward H. Shortliffe's thesis 'MYCIN: A Rule-Based Computer Program for Advising Physicians Regarding Antimicrobial Therapy Selection' as an October 1974 Ph.D. thesis and describes MYCIN as a rule-based problem-solving system for therapy selection in bacterial infections, including consultation, explanation, and rule-acquisition components. A later Stanford presentation by Shortliffe identifies the MYCIN Project as spanning 1972-1979. This candidate intentionally uses year precision and represents the documented thesis milestone only. It does not convert October 1974 into MYCIN's invention, project-start, deployment, or system-birth date; does not infer development geography beyond Stanford's role as the thesis institution; and adds no ancestry, influence, causality, universal-priority claim, or relationship edge.
